# Pruning roses

In a temperate garden roses are pruned while they are dormant, after the
hardest frosts and before the buds break.

## Steps

1. Wait for a dry day in late winter, when the buds are swelling but not yet open.
2. Remove dead, diseased and damaged stems at their base.
3. Cut out stems that cross or rub, keeping the centre of the bush open.
4. Shorten the remaining stems by about a third, to an outward-facing bud.
5. Make each cut at a slant, a few millimetres above the bud.
6. Clear the fallen leaves and clippings from around the plant.

## Notes

Climbing roses are pruned differently: tie the long stems in along their
support and shorten only the side shoots that flowered last year.

A late frost after pruning can blacken the newest buds. Leave a spare bud
below each cut so the stem can shoot again.
